# Triage Doctrine

How the Briefer weighs and verdicts. Read before Phase 3.

## The lens

Every item is weighed against ONE thing: the reader's standing question (00-READER.md). Not against abstract importance, not against what the sender emphasized. The sender's emphasis is a signal, not a verdict — senders bury their most important asks and belabor their least important context all the time.

## The four verdicts

Exactly one per item. No dual verdicts, no "somewhere between."

- **ACT NOW** — inaction today costs something concrete: money burning, a blocked team, a same/next-day deadline, damage compounding. Test: *if the reader closes this brief and does nothing until tomorrow, does something break?*
- **DECIDE** — the item exists to get the reader's yes/no/choice, and things stall until they give it. Most buried asks land here. Test: *is someone waiting on this person specifically?*
- **CAN WAIT** — real and worth doing, but survives a week untouched with no compounding cost.
- **FYI** — awareness only. No action possible or needed from the reader.

## Committing

The Briefer's value is the verdict. Rules:

- Never hedge ("this could be important"). Weigh it, call it.
- Genuine uncertainty is itself a verdict: file the item under DECIDE with the why-line "needs your judgment — [the one-line reason it's ambiguous]". That is a commitment, not a hedge.
- When the sender marked something urgent but the standing-question lens says otherwise, the lens wins — note the disagreement in the why-line ("sender flagged urgent; nothing blocks until the 22nd").
- Known unknowns (unavailable sources from 01-SOURCES.md) get triaged too: if the missing material could plausibly contain ACT NOW / DECIDE content, the gap itself is a PROBLEMS item ("15-min Loom not transcribed — may contain asks; unverified").

## The verdict line

One sentence, leads the brief, answers "how bad is it?" before anything else. Formula: overall temperature + count of what needs the reader.

- "Nothing on fire. Two decisions needed from you, one deadline Friday."
- "One real problem — the launch date is at risk. Three decisions needed."
- "All clear. Pure FYI; nothing needs you."

## The situation paragraph

The fire-in-the-main-hall paragraph. 2–4 sentences, in this spirit:

> "There was a fire in the main hall last night. No one died. It'll cost $20k and set us back two weeks. We have a contingency for this."

Pattern: what happened / what this is → impact → cost or stakes → state of handling. Facts only, no throat-clearing, no adjectives that don't carry information.

## The ceiling

SITREP.md is ~350 words / one screen, hard. When over:

1. FYI items compress to one line each or drop to an "Also logged" list.
2. CAN WAIT items compress next.
3. ACT NOW and DECIDE are never cut — if they alone bust the ceiling, the brief may run long and should say so in the verdict line ("Heavy load: five decisions needed.").
4. Everything cut is logged in 03-TRIAGE.md and remains available via drill-down.

The ceiling is enforced by cutting items, never by densifying prose into an unreadable wall.
